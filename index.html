I build design systems that teams choose to use.

For five years I led the Government of Alberta Design System. No team was ever required to use it. More than 80 product teams across 13 ministries did.

Make the system the obvious choice

Teams in Alberta could adopt any mature open-source component library or build their own. So we had to earn adoption through product quality, documentation and the support around it. The goal was a system so useful that building from scratch stopped being a reasonable choice.

I've spent twelve years between design and delivery: UX designer, front-end design lead, then product owner of the design system. Since August 2026 I've been a hands-on designer again, building coded prototypes on that system with AI tools. I'm based in Vancouver.

Selected work

  • Running a system 80+ teams chose

    The operating model behind the Alberta Design System: how we decided what to build, let teams contribute, shipped versions and measured adoption.

    68 production releases, a major version migration across three code stacks, and a scanner that replaced estimates with real adoption data.

  • Theming without forking

    Component-level design tokens that let a minister-priority service carry its own look and still receive every fix from the core system.

    Delivered before a fixed deadline, with no fork of the system.

  • Building on the system with AI tools

    Making the design system readable by AI tools, then using it to build a method for designing coded prototypes.

    Seven designers chose to adopt the method. Their shared prototype covers seven connected services.

  • How I lead

    Leading the design system team, founding a front-end design practice, hiring, and handing work on so it doesn't depend on me.
