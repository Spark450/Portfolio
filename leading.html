How I lead

I lead by making the work clear, making it easy for others to take part, and setting things up so they keep running without me.

The design system team

For five years I led a team of 7 to 10 developers, designers, QA and a digital architect. I owned the vision, roadmap and backlog, and worked in the same GitHub repository as the developers, reviewing and managing pull requests. I used the scored backlog to make trade-offs visible, so decisions held up even with teams whose requests had to wait.

Building a design practice

Before the design system, I founded the front-end design practice in Digital Design and Delivery: a guild of 30+, design pattern working groups, and role-specific onboarding. Until a UX director was appointed, I interviewed and made hiring recommendations on every front-end designer we brought in, while the division's UX organization grew from four people to about forty.

Bringing people in early

I'd rather find problems with users than after launch. The early adopter program put 7 ministry teams on the new version before release, and their feedback changed the docs and migration plan. I ran six research programs on how designers and developers actually used the system, One designer's question at drop-in hours turned into a partnership that tested our public form pattern with real users.

Handing work on

A design system shouldn't depend on one person. I designed a three-stage handover, understand, try and run, so the wider UX team could take over daily operations: support, working groups, newsletters and executive reporting. Each stage had clear criteria for moving to the next.

Explaining the why

I reported monthly to the Deputy Minister of Technology and Innovation, and kept four groups of stakeholders informed, from practitioners to executives. In fall 2026 I co-presented "Just Enough" at UX Camp with two design system teammates, on how the team decided, built and took contributions.
